Eropean stag beetle

Land Insect

The European stag beetle is the largest beetle in Europe. [6] Their colour is usually black with reddish elytra (and red mandibles in males).

Lucanus cervus, known as the European stag beetle, or the greater stag beetle, is one of the best-known species of stag beetle in Western Europe, and is the eponymous example of the genus. L. cervus is listed as Near Threatened by the IUCN Red List.

Mantis

Land Insect

A mantis is a carnivorous insect of the order Mantodea, famous for its folded, spiny front legs that resemble a praying posture.

Mantises are an order of insects that contains over 2,400 species in about 460 genera in 33 families. The largest family is the Mantidae. Mantises are distributed worldwide in temperate and tropical habitats. They have triangular heads with bulging eyes supported on flexible necks.

Handmaiden Moth

Land Insect

The handmaiden moth (Syntomoides imaon) is a striking, day-flying insect belonging to the subfamily Arctiinae (tiger and wasp moths) within the family Erebidae.

Syntomoides imaon, the handmaiden moth, is a moth of subfamily Arctiinae, subtribe Ctenuchina. The systematics of the subfamily has been revised. It was described by Pieter Cramer in 1780. It is found in Pakistan, India, Sri Lanka, Myanmar, Bangladesh, Nepal, China, Thailand and Vietnam.

Black Ant

Land Insect

The black garden ant (Lasius niger), also known as the common black ant, is a formicine ant, the type species of the genus Lasius, which is found across Europe.

The black garden ant, also known as the common black ant, is a formicine ant, the type species of the genus Lasius, which is found across Europe and in some parts of North America, and believed to inhabit certain parts of Asia.
